Technical analysis reads price behaviour on the chart. Rather than judging the business, it judges the market psychology printed onto price and volume.

## The basics worth mastering

Start with <Term slug="trend">Trend</Term>, the dominant direction of price. Moving with the trend is usually safer than fighting it. <Term slug="support-resistance">Support and Resistance</Term> mark the areas where price tends to turn.

Tools such as <Term slug="moving-average">Moving Average</Term> smooth the noise and sharpen direction, while <Term slug="rsi">RSI</Term> helps flag overbought or oversold conditions. Every one of those signals reads stronger when backed by adequate <Term slug="volume">Volume</Term>.

## Smart Money Concept

Smart Money Concept, or SMC, views the market through the movements of large institutions. The terminology stays in English because it is the industry standard.

The starting point is often <Term slug="liquidity">Liquidity</Term>, clusters of orders around obvious highs or lows that price tends to target. Nearby, an <Term slug="order-block">Order Block</Term> marks the footprint of large orders before a strong move, and a <Term slug="fair-value-gap">Fair Value Gap</Term> shows an imbalance that is often revisited.

To read structural direction, <Term slug="break-of-structure">Break of Structure</Term> suggests the trend likely continues, while <Term slug="change-of-character">Change of Character</Term> gives an early reversal signal. Finally, <Term slug="premium-discount">Premium and Discount</Term> helps judge whether current price sits in the expensive or cheap zone of a range.

## Bringing the two together

Technical analysis and SMC answer when and where, not what. Their strength appears when paired with solid fundamentals, then guarded by disciplined risk management.
