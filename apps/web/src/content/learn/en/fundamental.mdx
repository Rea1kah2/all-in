Fundamental analysis judges the business behind a stock. The goal is simple, to understand whether you are buying a good company at a reasonable price.

## Valuation, does the price make sense

A share price on its own is neither cheap nor expensive. What matters is price relative to performance. <Term slug="pe-ratio">P/E Ratio</Term> compares price to earnings per share, so you know what you pay for each dollar of profit. The value of the whole company shows up as <Term slug="market-cap">Market Cap</Term>.

Companies that return part of their profit to shareholders have a <Term slug="dividend-yield">Dividend Yield</Term>. Keep in mind that many growth companies deliberately pay no dividend and reinvest instead.

## Profitability, how well the business makes money

<Term slug="roe">ROE</Term> measures how efficiently a company turns shareholder capital into profit. Paired with <Term slug="eps">EPS</Term>, you get a picture of the earnings attributable to each share.

Profit on paper is not always cash. <Term slug="free-cash-flow">Free Cash Flow</Term> shows the real cash left after operating costs and capital spending, the money that can actually be used.

## Health and growth

Watch the debt structure through <Term slug="debt-to-equity">Debt to Equity</Term>. A ratio that runs too high leaves the company fragile when conditions turn. Finally, look at <Term slug="revenue-growth">Revenue Growth</Term> to judge whether the business is expanding or slowing.

Fundamentals answer what business you are buying. To decide when, continue to technical analysis.
